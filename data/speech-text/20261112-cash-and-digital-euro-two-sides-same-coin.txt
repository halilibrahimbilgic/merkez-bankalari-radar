SPEECH
Cash and the digital euro: two sides of the
same coin
Introductory statement by Piero Cipollone, Member of the Executive
Board of the ECB, at the Committee on Economic and Monetary
Affairs of the European Parliament
Brussels, 10 November 2026
It is a pleasure to be back here for the first time since the Parliament adopted its position on the Single
Currency Package, and I very much welcome the broad support it received in the plenary session on 9
July. This gives a strong democratic backing to the digital euro. Let me also thank you for the
considerable work and constructive dialogue that have made this progress possible.
A swift conclusion of the ongoing trilogue negotiations would pave the way for the adoption of the
Single Currency Package[ ] by the end of the year as foreseen in the One Europe, One Market
Roadmap.[ ] This would give us the legal certainty needed to complete our preparations for the digital
euro. It would also give certainty to the market regarding the planned date for a possible first issuance,
which can be set only after the Regulation is adopted. A delay in adoption could instead postpone the
introduction of the digital euro, further entrench Europe’s dependence on foreign providers, and make
it more difficult for European companies to innovate and compete.
Europe’s achievements in payments innovation go beyond progress on the digital euro. In September
we launched Pontes, which connects market distributed ledger technology (DLT) platforms with the
Eurosystem’s TARGET Services.[ ] The launch was widely welcomed by the market, with 18 market
participants and market DLT operators onboarded on day one and many others preparing to join soon.
Pontes makes the settlement of tokenised transactions in central bank money an operational reality
and is a major milestone in the development of digital European capital markets. On 2 November it
settled the issuance of the European Stability Mechanism’s first digitally native benchmark bond of €1
billion[ ], facilitating the shift in European tokenised finance from experimentation to adoption.
While I am happy to answer any questions on Pontes, I would like to focus my remarks today on the
digital euro and cash.
Preparing for the digital euro pilot and first issuance
We are moving ahead with preparations for the digital euro pilot. It will test the digital euro’s technical
functionality, operational processes and user experience, using a beta version without legal tender
status.
Starting in the second half of 2027, the pilot will involve Eurosystem staff, participating payment
service providers (PSPs) and selected merchants offering everyday services – for example in

We have seen strong market interest in the pilot and, in July, we announced the 36 PSPs selected to
join the pilot from more than 50 applicants.[ ] They include banks and non-bank providers, and cover a
broad range of business models, sizes and geographical locations.
We are working closely with participating PSPs to hear their views and benefit from their experience.[ ]
In September we opened an additional call for online and mobile merchants to join the pilot to test
remote payments. The final selection of merchants will be announced early next year.[ ]
This collaboration with market players is essential. The digital euro must work on a technical level, but
also in everyday life – for consumers, merchants and PSPs.
Looking further ahead, we are planning for the digital euro rollout.
On our side, we plan to ensure that all relevant use cases[ ] for the digital euro are made technically
available from first issuance. At the same time, feedback from market participants[ ] shows that a
gradual rollout would support a smooth and successful first issuance by giving them sufficient time to
adapt. We are glad that the co-legislators intend to provide legal clarity on the feasibility of such a
phased rollout in shops.
In preparation for first issuance, we are also working more closely with the market on the digital euro
innovation platform.
In September we launched a new call for expression of interest, inviting private companies and
organisations to explore how the digital euro can foster market innovation.[ ] The next wave of
activities, including testing conditional payments and exploring applications of artificial intelligence in
payments, will run from January to June 2027.[ ]
Redesigning euro banknotes
Innovation also extends to banknotes. In July we invited Europeans to share their views on ten
shortlisted design proposals for the new euro banknotes based on the themes “European culture” and
“Rivers and birds”.
The survey received almost 10 million responses, with high participation across all euro area countries
and age groups.[ ] This demonstrates how deeply Europeans care about their money.
The survey results will inform the ECB Governing Council’s decision on the final design concept,
expected around the end of this year. At that point, we will also publish the survey results. First
denominations of the new banknote series are expected to enter into circulation in the early 2030s.[ ]
But cash must also remain widely available and accepted in practice. We therefore support strong
legislative provisions on mandatory cash acceptance, access to cash across the euro area and the
resilience of cash infrastructure.
Conclusion
Let me conclude.

In today’s uncertain geopolitical environment, timely progress on the digital euro is essential to
strengthen Europe’s autonomy in payments. We are doing our part by progressing with the technical
preparations. But the adoption of the legal framework is necessary to finalise the digital euro’s design
and Rulebook[ ] and, ultimately, to issue the digital euro.
In two months’ time we will mark the 25th anniversary of euro banknotes and coins entering
circulation. While cash is still widely used in shops, digital payments are growing. I therefore hope that
by then we will have a strong legal framework in place: one that both safeguards the acceptance of
cash and gives Europeans a digital form of cash alongside it.
Thank you.
